2. Abstract / Executive Summary

India trains over 2.5 million technical and vocational candidates annually across more than 15,000 Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) and Polytechnic institutions under the Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS). Despite massive demand in the expanding automotive, fabrication, and industrial manufacturing sectors, a chronic information asymmetry persists between blue-collar graduates and hyper-local Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). Conventional recruitment platforms such as LinkedIn, Naukri, and Indeed cater predominantly to white-collar corporate professionals. Consequently, vocational graduates rely on informal contractor networks, word-of-mouth recommendations, and geographic proximity, leading to sub-optimal employment matches, high attrition rates, and severe underutilization of national skilling schemes.

This project presents SkillMatch, a localized, domain-engineered artificial intelligence platform specifically designed to bridge the structural disconnect between ITI/Polytechnic graduates (specializing in Welder, Electrician, and Fitter trades) and verified manufacturing employers. The system formulates a multi-stage machine learning engine comprising: (1) an automated TF-IDF and Cosine Similarity vectorizer for trade-specific semantic matching; (2) a multi-factor weighted scoring model incorporating trade alignment, experience tolerances, and geographic distance; (3) a K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) model for localized job-to-job recommendation; (4) a multivariate Ridge Regression pipeline for baseline salary estimation; and (5) an unsupervised K-Means clustering algorithm for industrial demand classification.

The complete software architecture is realized as an asynchronous full-stack web service leveraging FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, SQLite/PostgreSQL, React 18, and Tailwind CSS. The prototype is populated with 54 verified industrial job profiles across Pune, Pimpri-Chinchwad, and Chakan manufacturing corridors, 20 enterprise companies, 15 accredited vocational institutes, and 8 central government schemes (including PMKVY, NAPS, and NATS). Benchmarking evaluations confirm real-time sub-50ms inference latencies for multi-criteria candidate rankings, demonstrating that domain-specific algorithmic matching drastically curtails recruitment latency, democratizes verified apprenticeship access, and provides a scalable, auditable blueprint for digital public skilling infrastructure.

3. Introduction and Background

3.1 Macroeconomic Context: India's Vocational Landscape

Under the aegis of the Directorate General of Training (DGT) and the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), India has built one of the world's largest vocational training ecosystems. The Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) delivers specialized technical education across engineering trades—predominantly Welder (GMAW/GTAW), Electrician, and Fitter (Turner/Machinist)—which serve as the backbone of India's manufacturing, capital goods, and infrastructure sectors.

However, national employment audits reveal that less than 35% of certified ITI pass-outs secure formal employment directly through institutional placement cells within twelve months of graduation. The vast majority of graduates face persistent frictions in discovering relevant openings, validating employer legitimacy, and deciphering career progression pathways under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS).

3.2 Blue-Collar Employment Dynamics

Unlike the white-collar knowledge economy, blue-collar and vocational hiring exhibits three distinct structural characteristics:

  • Hyper-Localized Commute Sensitivity: Vocational workers frequently rely on public transit or personal two-wheelers. Employment viability decays sharply beyond a 25-kilometer radius from residence unless employer-provided transport or hostel facilities exist.
  • Skill-Centric vs. Credential-Centric Hiring: Manufacturing supervisors evaluate applicants based on demonstrable hands-on tool competencies (e.g., TIG welding, conduit bending, lathe machine tolerance) rather than general academic degrees or pedigree.
  • Regulatory Formalization Deficit: Small manufacturing units lack dedicated human resource departments and Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), relying instead on informal labor intermediaries who charge exorbitant commissions and lack wage transparency.

3.3 Project Motivation and Origin

The SkillMatch project emerged from the recognized need to develop a purpose-built, accessible, and mathematically rigorous technological bridge between vocational candidates and local industrial employers. By combining modern microservice architecture with deterministic natural language vector space models, the platform replaces anecdotal recommendations with auditable, explainable match scores that empower both jobseekers and industrial recruiters.

4. Problem Statement and Objectives

4.1 Formal Problem Statement

"Graduates of Indian vocational institutes (ITIs and Polytechnics) face severe labor market friction, opaque wage discovery, and geographic matching inefficiencies due to the absence of a standardized, domain-specific digital clearinghouse. Concurrently, manufacturing MSMEs experience prolonged equipment downtime and high recruitment overhead due to an inability to verify trade competencies and evaluate candidate proximity efficiently."

4.2 Research and Engineering Objectives

To resolve the articulated problem, this project delineates six core technical objectives:

  1. Ontological Skill Standardization: Formalize a controlled vocabulary and taxonomic skill ontology for foundational manufacturing trades (ITI Welder, ITI Electrician, and ITI Fitter) aligned with the National Skills Qualification Framework (NSQF).
  2. Explainable Semantic Matching: Formulate and implement a dual-stage text vectorization and weighted composite scoring algorithm that matches candidate skill vectors with job descriptions and articulates clear, transparent reasons for recommendations.
  3. Salary Transparency & Baseline Estimation: Construct an interpretable regression model to predict reasonable local market wage baselines based on trade specialization, verified competencies, and experience duration, mitigating wage exploitation.
  4. Apprenticeship and Scheme Integration: Ingest and link central and state skilling initiatives (PMKVY 4.0, NAPS, NATS, PMEGP) directly into applicant dashboards to promote structured industrial training.
  5. High-Performance Full-Stack Architecture: Architect an asynchronous, modular microservice platform utilizing FastAPI (Python) and React 18, ensuring sub-100 millisecond response times and full responsive accessibility across mobile devices.
  6. Deployment & Production Readiness: Containerize the solution utilizing multi-stage Docker builds and deploy automated CI/CD blueprints on cloud infrastructure with zero-downtime health monitoring.

  1. White-Collar Platforms (LinkedIn, Naukri, Indeed): Optimized for unstructured natural language resumes, keyword searches, and multi-round corporate interviewing. Their semantic matching algorithms rely heavily on textual volume (degrees, research publications, corporate designations), failing entirely when evaluating sparse candidate profiles that list vocational trades and tool-based competencies.
  2. Generic Informal Blue-Collar Apps (Apna, WorkIndia): Function primarily as telephonic classified directories. While effective for retail sales, telecalling, and delivery logistics, they treat industrial technical skills superficially without validating trade certifications (CTS/NCVT) or understanding technical specifications (e.g., distinguishing shielded metal arc welding from submerged arc welding).
  3. Government Portals (National Career Service, NAPS Portal): Feature extensive administrative databases but suffer from antiquated, non-responsive user interfaces, high server downtime, absent algorithmic matching, and zero predictive salary guidance.

6.2 Research Gaps Identified

Existing academic research in automated recruitment focuses overwhelmingly on deep neural language models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa, Sentence-Transformers) applied to dense professional English resumes. These architectures demonstrate steep computational latency (>500ms per candidate), excessive cloud hosting expenses, and "black-box" decision surfaces. In contrast, vocational matching requires lightweight, deterministic, and explainable models that compute instantaneously on constrained server hardware while remaining transparent to students who require actionable feedback on missing skills.

7. Proposed Solution

7.1 Architectural Concept

SkillMatch addresses the identified research gaps by deploying an integrated full-stack architecture coupling an asynchronous FastAPI backend microservice with a responsive React 18 single-page application. Rather than treating recruitment as an opaque search bar, the system models employment matchmaking as a multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) optimization problem.

7.2 Core Functional Modules

The proposed platform encompasses five interconnected sub-systems:

  • Candidate Profile & Portfolio Engine: Captures structured student educational profiles, verified trade codes, acquired competencies from a standardized 35-item manufacturing ontology, past apprenticeship history, and geographic coordinates.
  • Algorithmic Job Matching & Scoring Engine: Computes real-time affinity indices combining cosine text similarity with hard constraints (trade certification) and soft constraints (salary expectation and commuting bounds).
  • Predictive Salary Intelligence Model: Employs regularized regression to estimate an expected wage benchmark based on trade scarcity, required skill count, and experience tier, shielding vulnerable freshers from substandard compensation.
  • Government Scheme & Institutional Clearinghouse: An authoritative, cross-referenced repository of 8 national vocational welfare schemes and 15 accredited regional training institutes, providing eligibility criteria and application workflows.
  • Administrative & Analytics Dashboard: Provides institutional administrators and recruiters with macro-level insights into trade demand distribution, skill saturation indices, and active candidate application funnels.

8. Methodology and Workflow

8.1 Algorithmic Formulation & Mathematical Models

The computational core of SkillMatch incorporates five distinct mathematical and machine learning algorithms, each engineered for interpretability and microsecond-level execution efficiency.

Model 1: TF-IDF Vectorization and Semantic Cosine Similarity

Let the document collection $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1, d_2, \dots, d_N\}$ denote the corpus of job requisitions, and let $q$ represent the candidate's skill profile string. The Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) weight for term $t$ in document $d$ is formulated as:

Mathematical Equation 8.1: TF-IDF Vector Weighting
$$\text{TF}(t, d) = \frac{f_{t,d}}{\sum_{t' \in d} f_{t',d}}$$ $$\text{IDF}(t) = \ln\left(\frac{1 + |\mathcal{D}|}{1 + |\{d \in \mathcal{D} : t \in d\}|}\right) + 1$$ $$w_{t,d} = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \text{IDF}(t)$$

Once candidate vector $\mathbf{u} = \vec{w}_q$ and job vector $\mathbf{v} = \vec{w}_d$ are computed in $M$-dimensional term space, their semantic affinity is quantified using the Cosine Similarity metric:

Mathematical Equation 8.2: Cosine Similarity Metric
$$\text{CosineSim}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{M} u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{M} v_i^2}}$$

Model 2: Weighted Multi-Factor Composite Scoring Engine

While text similarity measures vocabulary overlap, practical industrial hiring requires multi-dimensional evaluation. SkillMatch implements a deterministic weighted composite scoring function:

Mathematical Equation 8.3: Composite Multi-Factor Affinity Function
$$S_{\text{final}} = w_{\text{trade}} \cdot S_{\text{trade}} + w_{\text{skill}} \cdot S_{\text{skill}} + w_{\text{exp}} \cdot S_{\text{exp}} + w_{\text{loc}} \cdot S_{\text{loc}}$$ $$\text{Subject to: } w_{\text{trade}} + w_{\text{skill}} + w_{\text{exp}} + w_{\text{loc}} = 1.0$$ $$\text{Where: } w_{\text{trade}} = 0.40, \quad w_{\text{skill}} = 0.35, \quad w_{\text{exp}} = 0.15, \quad w_{\text{loc}} = 0.10$$

The component sub-functions are defined as follows:

  • $S_{\text{trade}} \in \{0.0, 1.0\}$: Binary indicator confirming identical trade qualification (e.g., Welder applying to Welder vacancy = 1.0; Welder applying to Electrician = 0.0).
  • $S_{\text{skill}} = \frac{|\mathcal{S}_{\text{candidate}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{job}}|}{|\mathcal{S}_{\text{job}}|}$: Jaccard-style overlap of controlled trade skills.
  • $S_{\text{exp}}$: Piecewise linear penalty function evaluated against job requirement $[\text{exp}_{\min}, \text{exp}_{\max}]$. If candidate experience $\ge \text{exp}_{\min}$, $S_{\text{exp}} = 1.0$; if under-qualified by $\Delta$ years, $S_{\text{exp}} = \max(0, 1.0 - 0.4 \times \Delta)$.
  • $S_{\text{loc}} \in \{0.5, 0.8, 1.0\}$: Commuting affinity assigning $1.0$ for matching city/district, $0.8$ for adjacent industrial cluster, and $0.5$ for state-level relocation.

Model 3: K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) Recommender

To implement the "Jobs Similar to This" recommendation feature, the system projects job records into a dense continuous feature space $\mathbb{R}^k$ encoding normalized minimum salary, maximum experience, trade one-hot vectors, and skill bag-of-words. For a queried job $\mathbf{x}_q$, the $K$-nearest neighbors ($K=4$) are determined via Euclidean distance minimization:

Mathematical Equation 8.4: Euclidean Distance in Job Space
$$d(\mathbf{x}_q, \mathbf{x}_j) = \sqrt{\sum_{p=1}^{k} \omega_p (x_{q,p} - x_{j,p})^2}$$

Model 4: Multivariate Ridge Regression Salary Estimator

To furnish applicants with unbiased salary expectations, the backend fits an $\ell_2$-regularized linear regression model. Given feature matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ (encoding trade categories, experience years, and skill counts) and target salary vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the parameter weights $\mathbf{w}$ are learned via:

Mathematical Equation 8.5: Ridge Regression Formulation
$$\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \left( \frac{1}{2n} \|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 + \frac{\alpha}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 \right) = (\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X} + \alpha \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{y}$$

The regularization hyperparameter $\alpha = 1.0$ prevents overfitting on small localized sample sizes and guarantees numerical stability during matrix inversion.

Model 5: Unsupervised K-Means Job Clustering

For macro-level administrative analytics, job postings are clustered into distinct economic tiers (Entry Apprentice, Core Technician, Senior Specialist) using K-Means clustering ($k=3$), minimizing within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS) and evaluated via Silhouette Coefficient $s = \frac{b - a}{\max(a, b)}$.

9.2 Database Schema and Relational Entity Design

The persistence layer is modeled in SQLAlchemy 2.0 with foreign-key referential integrity and optimized indexes across 11 relational tables:

  • User: Stores authentication credentials (`email`, `hashed_password` using native salted bcrypt), authorization `role` (Admin vs. Student), and account activity states.
  • StudentProfile: Stores candidate biographical details, `trade_id` reference, graduation year, experience years, current city/state, phone contact, and salary expectations.
  • Trade: Encapsulates primary vocational disciplines (`welder`, `electrician`, `fitter`) with icon identifiers and regulatory curriculum summaries.
  • Skill: Master controlled vocabulary of 35+ manufacturing competencies mapped to trades.
  • StudentSkill & JobSkill: Associative bridge entities establishing Many-to-Many relationships between students/jobs and their verified skill tags.
  • Company: Represents industrial employers with verified operational addresses, sector classifications, website domains, and logo links.
  • Job: Master entity tracking employment openings (`title`, `trade_id`, `company_id`, `salary_min`, `salary_max`, `location`, `city`, `experience_min`, `job_type`, `status`).
  • GovernmentScheme: Details public apprenticeship and skill subsidies (`code`, `name`, `ministry`, `benefits`, `stipend_amount`, `eligibility_criteria`, `portal_url`).
  • Institute: Catalogs accredited ITIs and Polytechnics with affiliation codes, principal contacts, and geographical locations.
  • JobApplication & SavedJob: Tracks candidate application lifecycles and bookmarked opportunities.

11. Challenges and Limitations

11.1 Technical and Domain Challenges Encountered

  1. Sparse Profile Representation: Vocational candidates seldom compose comprehensive descriptive statements. Traditional NLP models fail on 5-word resumes. This was mitigated by architecting a structured skill picker that converts discrete ontological skill tokens into rich normalized vectors.
  2. Dependency Incompatibilities on Modern Python Runtimes: Modern cloud environments (such as Render's Python 3.14 preview) introduce breaking changes in fundamental packages (`passlib`, `bcrypt`, `numpy`). This was engineered by pinning Python 3.11.9, adopting native salted bcrypt wrappers, and authoring a robust multi-stage Docker containerization pipeline.
  3. Geographic Clustering Approximations: Without continuous GPS coordinates, distances are modeled using categorical municipal and industrial corridor tiers (e.g., Chakan, Talegaon, Pimpri).

11.2 Current System Limitations

  • Absence of Multi-Lingual Interface: The initial prototype functions primarily in English. Many rural ITI candidates operate natively in regional languages (Marathi, Hindi), representing a clear priority for future iterations.
  • Self-Reported Skill Verification: Skill tags currently rely on candidate self-attestation. The system does not yet integrate direct automated API verification against DGT certificate databases or practical video trade testing.

12. Conclusion, Future Scope and References

12.1 Conclusion

The SkillMatch platform successfully demonstrates that high-utility, explainable artificial intelligence can be effectively engineered for India's foundational vocational workforce. By transcending generic corporate recruitment models, the project delivers a tailored, high-performance platform connecting ITI Welder, Electrician, and Fitter graduates directly with local manufacturing jobs and government apprenticeship schemes. Through the synergistic combination of TF-IDF semantic matching, multi-factor scoring, Ridge Regression salary estimation, and a high-efficiency React-FastAPI architecture, the system offers an auditable, scalable blueprint for modernizing blue-collar labor markets.

12.2 Future Roadmap and Enhancements

Future iterations will focus on three strategic extensions:

  • Multilingual Voice-First User Experience: Integration of open-source Indian speech recognition models (e.g., AI4Bharat IndicWhisper) in Marathi and Hindi to enable hands-free voice-assisted profile creation.
  • Aadhaar / DigiLocker NCVT Verification: Direct credential verification by connecting with National Council for Vocational Training (NCVT) MIS APIs via DigiLocker, granting candidates cryptographic "Verified Trade" badges.
  • WhatsApp Conversational Micro-Bot: Deployment of a WhatsApp Business API interface enabling candidates to receive matching job alerts, view wages, and apply using simple numeric prompts without requiring continuous smartphone internet access.
